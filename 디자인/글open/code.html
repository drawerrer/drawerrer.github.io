The Metaphysics of
Digital Decay

November 14th, 1994

Everything that is built eventually succumbs to the patient hunger of time. In the physical realm, we see this as rust, as crumbling brick, as the slow encroaching of ivy upon forgotten walls. But what of the digital? We were promised a medium that was eternal, a perfect preservation of thought that would never yellow like paper or rot like wood. We were misled.

The degradation of a file is a silent, invisible tragedy. Bits flip in the dark of a spinning platter; magnetic charges weaken; links rot as the servers that anchored them vanish into the ether of bankruptcy and obsolescence.

Observed remnants of the first generation

We are now the curators of a vanishing library. Our archives are not made of stone but of electricity, a fragile current that must be constantly maintained to exist. If the power fails, if the format shifts, if the keys are lost—the knowledge ceases to be. It does not wait in the dust like a Dead Sea Scroll; it simply evaporates.

"The ghost in the machine is not a spirit, but the sound of the machine slowly failing."

To write is to resist this decay. To print is to surrender to a different kind of ending, one that at least leaves a corpse behind. As I look at these files, these digital 'scrolls' I have accumulated, I wonder which of them will be legible to the eyes of a century from now.

adjust

Rowan
